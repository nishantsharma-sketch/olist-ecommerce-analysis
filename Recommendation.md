# A Few Things I'd Suggest Based on This Data

### Just some thoughts based on patterns I found while working through this project - not a full business strategy, just what stood out.
____
**1. Don't rely on one state so much.** SP alone makes up about 42% of orders and 37% of revenue, more than 3x any other state. But it actually has the lowest average order value of all states, so it's mostly about order count, not bigger purchases. Probably safer to grow the next few states too instead of depending on one region this much.

**2. Repeat customers are really rare.** Only about 3% of customers ever placed a second order. That was honestly the most surprising thing I found - almost all the growth is from new customers, not people coming back. Even a basic reminder email or small loyalty perk might change that a bit.

**3. Delivery time seems to affect ratings more than anything else.** Once delivery crosses somewhere around 15-20 days, review scores start dropping pretty clearly. I looked this up a bit, and Olist's overall average delivery is usually around 12 days, but that gap between states is huge - fast states are close to 7-10 days while some remote ones stretch to 30. From what I found, this kind of regional gap isn't unique to Olist either, it's a pretty well-known issue with e-commerce logistics in Brazil in general because of how spread out and hard to reach some regions are. So it's less a "fix everything" problem and more about focusing on the specific slow regions first.

**4. Almost everyone pays by credit card.** Boleto, debit, and voucher barely get used. Might be worth testing some kind of incentive for the other payment options so it's not this dependent on just one method.
